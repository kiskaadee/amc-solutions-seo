# AMC Solutions — Stakeholder Discovery Brief

Create a concise stakeholder-facing discovery brief for AMC Solutions Colombia.

The document combines the most relevant findings from:

1. The technical exploration of AMC's website.
2. The external presence exploration.

## Purpose

This document will be sent to AMC representatives to read before a potential discovery meeting.

Its purpose is to establish a shared factual baseline, communicate what has been observed about AMC's externally visible digital presence, clarify what cannot be established through external research alone, and identify the information needed to continue the project responsibly.

It is not a technical audit, SEO report, marketing proposal, or list of recommended improvements.

The document must be self-contained: AMC should be able to understand the findings and their relevance without the researcher being present to explain them. At the same time, it should prepare the ground for a subsequent conversation rather than attempt to resolve every uncertainty in writing.

## Content and progression

The document should progress naturally through the following stages:

1. **What we observed:** Present the most relevant findings from the website and external presence explorations.
2. **What those observations establish:** Explain what can reasonably be concluded from the evidence, without overstating its significance.
3. **What remains unknown:** Identify the uncertainties that external observation cannot resolve and distinguish them from established findings.
4. **Why clarification matters:** Explain how resolving these uncertainties would improve the accuracy and relevance of the next phase of research. Do not invent business consequences or use speculative implications to make findings appear more significant.
5. **Questions for AMC:** Conclude with a small number of purposeful questions whose answers could materially change the interpretation of the findings or the direction of subsequent work.

These stages should form a coherent narrative, not five mechanically separated sections. Group related findings together and introduce questions where they naturally follow from the preceding discussion.

## Selection and level of detail

Do not attempt to report every observation. Select only findings that materially contribute to understanding AMC's current digital presence, externally observable visibility, or the uncertainties that affect the next project decision.

Prefer concise paragraphs and short bullet points over technical exposition.

Include enough context for each finding to be understood independently. Where a finding has an important limitation, keep that limitation close to the finding rather than relegating it to a general disclaimer.

Exclude internal research identifiers, raw technical output, discarded search results, implementation details, and methodological explanations unless they are necessary to understand a substantive finding.

## Epistemic integrity

Clearly distinguish between:

- **Observed facts:** What was directly observed in the inspected material.
- **Source claims:** What a third-party source reports, without automatically treating it as independently verified truth.
- **Bounded interpretations:** What the observations reasonably suggest, with their limitations preserved.
- **Unresolved questions:** What remains unconfirmed and requires additional evidence or clarification from AMC.

The document must preserve the epistemic status of every finding.

The communication layer may change:

- Ordering and grouping.
- Wording for readability.
- Level of detail.
- Visual emphasis and transitions.
- Framing appropriate to the audience.

It may not change:

- Factual meaning.
- Uncertainty or confidence.
- Temporal scope.
- Evidence status or attribution.
- The distinction between observation, source claim, interpretation, and hypothesis.

In particular:

- Do not present historical evidence as current reality.
- Do not treat the absence of a result in a search sample as proof that something does not exist.
- Do not attribute ambiguous third-party profiles or records to AMC.
- Do not convert an unverified interpretation into an established finding.
- Do not infer business performance, customer behavior, commercial effectiveness, website traffic, search rankings, conversion rates, or strategic priorities without supporting evidence or explicit information from AMC.

Do not conceal limitations to make the document more persuasive, and do not exaggerate limitations to make routine observations appear more serious.

## Business relevance and decision boundaries

Explain why a finding matters only when its relevance is supported by the project objective or when the potential implication is explicitly framed as an uncertainty to investigate.

It is appropriate to explain why understanding AMC's actual service priorities, contact workflows, public presence, or operating context is necessary before evaluating possible improvements.

It is not appropriate to prescribe changes, assign commercial consequences, or imply that a particular solution is necessary before the relevant problem and business context have been established.

Do not tell AMC what it should do yet.

The purpose at this stage is to improve the accuracy of the shared understanding, not to sell a solution.

## Questions for AMC

The questions are the culmination of the document, not a disconnected questionnaire.

Select approximately two to four high-value questions. Include more only if the evidence establishes a compelling need.

Each question must:

- Be understandable without technical knowledge.
- Be answerable by an appropriate AMC representative.
- Avoid presupposing a problem or preferred answer.
- Distinguish current reality from historical practice or future plans where relevant.
- Address a specific unresolved uncertainty grounded in the research.
- Have a clear reason for being asked: different answers should materially affect the next research step or interpretation.
- Provide enough context for the reader to understand why the question matters.

Prefer open-ended questions about AMC's actual business, priorities, channels, and workflows over questions about technical tools or researcher-defined categories.

Do not force answers into artificial multiple-choice alternatives. Accept that AMC may provide information outside the researcher's anticipated possibilities.

The questions should prepare AMC for a productive conversation, not make the document feel like an administrative form.

## Audience and tone

Write for AMC representatives who understand the company's business and operations but may not be familiar with website architecture, SEO terminology, or technical web systems.

The document should feel like something a professional consultant would genuinely send to a client before a discovery meeting: clear, calm, intentional, direct, and useful.

Do not sound like an AI-generated report.

Avoid:

- Generic consulting language.
- Promotional or sales-oriented language.
- Exaggerated problem statements.
- Unnecessary methodology.
- Long explanations of the research process.
- Technical jargon used for its own sake.
- SEO terminology unless it materially helps explain a finding.
- Networking or infrastructure terminology unless it materially affects the interpretation.
- Marketing terminology unless necessary to describe an observed business or communication issue.

When a technical concept is necessary, introduce it organically in plain language on first mention. Retain the technical term only if it continues to help the reader.

Do not use formulaic expressions such as:

- "En el presente documento..."
- "Cabe destacar..."
- "Es importante señalar..."
- "Como parte de nuestra exhaustiva investigación..."
- "Se ha identificado una oportunidad..."
- "Esto representa un desafío significativo..."

Prefer concrete statements and natural transitions. Write as someone who has actually investigated AMC's digital presence and is now explaining the findings to its representatives.

## Communication objective

Optimize the document for one immediate outcome:

AMC should be able to read it before a potential meeting, understand what the research has actually established, recognize what remains uncertain, and arrive prepared to provide information that materially improves the next phase of the project.

The longer-term objective is to establish a sufficiently accurate understanding of AMC's actual business, priorities, clients, channels, and operating context to support a defensible proposal rather than one based on assumptions.

Do not optimize the document for demonstrating how much research was performed.

Optimize it for producing a better shared understanding and more useful information from AMC.

## Deliverable

Produce the complete stakeholder-facing document in Spanish, ready for editorial review and subsequent publication.

Keep it concise enough to read comfortably in one sitting. Prefer clarity and relevance over completeness.

Do not generate a separate technical appendix, additional questionnaire, or recommendations document unless explicitly requested.

Before delivering the draft, verify that every substantive claim is supported by admissible evidence, every limitation remains attached to the finding it qualifies, and every question addresses an uncertainty that matters to the next phase of the project.


### Document Architecture

Organize the brief into three parts, following this page structure:

#### Page 1 — Purpose and Initial Picture
Briefly explain what was examined, why the exploration was conducted, and what kind of understanding it aims to establish. Introduce the principal findings without trying to summarize every technical detail.

#### Pages 2–3 — Findings
Group observations under three themes:

1. **Services and contact channels:** how AMC presents its services and what contact mechanisms are observable.
2. **Technical signals on the website:** the relevant technical and structural characteristics visible in the inspected pages.
3. **Externally observable presence:** public records, directories, platforms, and other external references attributable to AMC, distinguishing confirmed attribution from unresolved candidates.

Keep the most relevant limitations beside each finding. Prioritize findings that materially contribute to understanding AMC's current digital presence; do not force every observation into the brief.

#### Final Page — Questions and Discussion
Present three or four prioritized questions, each connected to something already discussed in the brief and to an uncertainty that AMC can help resolve.

End by establishing the intended next step: validating the current picture with AMC before defining the scope of further analysis.

Treat page allocations as structural targets, not permission to omit necessary qualifications, alter findings, or manipulate content to fit. Preserve the document's factual meaning and epistemic status throughout.
