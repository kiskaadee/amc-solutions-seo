Create a concise stakeholder-facing discovery brief for AMC Solutions Colombia.

The document combines the most relevant findings from:

1. the technical exploration of AMC's website;
2. the external presence exploration.

Its purpose is not to provide a technical audit, SEO report, marketing proposal, or list of recommended improvements.

Its immediate purpose is to help AMC understand what has been observed from the outside, establish a shared factual baseline, make the limits of that external view clear, and obtain the information required to continue the project responsibly.

The document should naturally progress through:

1. What we observed
2. What those observations tell us
3. What cannot be established from external observation alone
4. Why resolving those knowledge gaps matters before defining a proposal
5. Questions for AMC

Keep the findings concise. Prefer short bullet points over technical exposition.

Do not attempt to report every observation. Select only findings that materially contribute to understanding AMC's current digital presence, external visibility, or the uncertainties that affect the next project decision.

The document should feel like something a professional consultant would actually send to a client before a discovery meeting: clear, calm, intentional, concise, and useful.

Do not sound like an AI-generated report.

Avoid:
- generic consulting language;
- promotional language;
- exaggerated problem statements;
- unnecessary methodology;
- long explanations of the research process;
- technical jargon used for its own sake;
- SEO terminology unless it materially helps explain a finding;
- networking/infrastructure terminology unless it materially affects a decision;
- marketing terminology unless it is necessary to describe an observed business or communication issue.

When a technical concept is genuinely necessary, introduce it organically in plain language on first mention and use the technical term only if it remains useful.

Do not tell AMC what they should do yet.

Do not infer business performance, customer behavior, commercial effectiveness, traffic, rankings, conversion rates, or strategic priorities unless directly supported by evidence or explicitly provided by AMC.

Clearly distinguish:
- observed facts;
- bounded interpretations;
- unresolved questions.

The document must preserve the epistemic status of every finding.

The communication layer may change:
- ordering;
- grouping;
- wording for readability;
- level of detail;
- visual emphasis;
- transitions;
- framing.

It may not change:
- factual meaning;
- uncertainty;
- temporal scope;
- evidence status;
- attribution;
- confidence.

### Communication objective

Optimize the document for one short-term outcome:

AMC should be able to read it quickly, understand what has actually been learned so far, recognize which parts cannot be determined externally, and provide useful answers to the questions that will materially improve the next phase of the project.

The longer-term objective is to establish enough understanding of AMC's actual business, priorities, clients, channels, and operating context to make a defensible proposal rather than proposing changes based on assumptions.

Do not optimize the document for demonstrating how much research was performed.

Optimize it for producing better information from AMC.

### Questionnaire

The questionnaire is the culmination of the document, not a disconnected appendix.

Select only questions whose answers can materially change the interpretation of the findings or the direction of the next phase.

Prefer a small number of high-value questions over exhaustive coverage.

Questions should:
- be understandable without technical knowledge;
- be answerable by AMC;
- avoid presupposing the answer;
- distinguish current reality from historical or planned situations where relevant;
- explain naturally why the information matters when necessary;
- avoid artificial multiple-choice framing unless genuinely useful.

The reader should understand why each question is being asked.

### Tone

Use a professional, direct, human tone.

Do not use:
- "En el presente documento..."
- "Cabe destacar..."
- "Es importante señalar..."
- "Como parte de nuestra exhaustiva investigación..."
- "Se ha identificado una oportunidad..."
- "Esto representa un desafío significativo..."
- generic executive-consulting filler.

Prefer concrete statements.

Write as someone who has actually investigated the company and is now speaking to its representatives.

The final artifact should be concise enough to read comfortably in one sitting and useful enough that AMC's responses can directly inform the next phase of the project.
