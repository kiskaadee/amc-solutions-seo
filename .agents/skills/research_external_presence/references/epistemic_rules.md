# Epistemic Rules for External Evidence

## Objective

Distinguish what is directly seen from what third parties assert, what independent sources corroborate, what inferences imply, and what remains unknown.

## Epistemic Taxonomy

Every entry in the research working set must classify findings into distinct categories:

### 1. Direct Observation (Observacion directa)
What the researcher directly observes on the inspected third-party resource at the time of retrieval.
- Example: "The directory listing displays the phone number +57 3136216458."
- Example: "The inspected registry entry displays registration date 2020-03-15 and status Activa."
- Principle: Observing X on source Y establishes that source Y displays or asserts X, not that X is independently established as objective reality.

### 2. Source Claim (Afirmacion de la fuente)
Assertions made by a third party about facts not directly verifiable from the resource itself.
- Example: "The directory profile states the company employs 25 engineers."
- Rule: Record what the source claims without adopting the claim as a verified fact.

### 3. Corroboration (Corroboracion)
Verification of an attribute or claim across two or more independent, non-affiliated sources.
- An uncorroborated claim remains classified as a single-source claim.
- Shared syndication (e.g. data scraped from the same underlying directory) does not constitute independent corroboration.

### 4. Interpretation (Interpretacion)
Hypotheses, analytical models, or potential implications drawn from recorded observations and claims.
- Interpretations must reference supporting observations.
- Interpretations must not introduce unsupported factual assertions.
- Example: "The absence of a claimed Google Business Profile may limit local pack visibility for branded search in Valledupar."

### 5. Unresolved Questions (Preguntas abiertas)
Explicitly identified gaps, contradictions between sources, or missing information requiring primary verification with the client or organization.

## Provenance and Temporal Scope

Every finding must record:
1. **Source identifier and URL:** Canonical location where the resource was viewed.
2. **Retrieval date:** ISO date when the researcher accessed the resource.
3. **Publication or update date:** Date stated by the source for when the information was published, updated, or filed.
4. **Temporal validity:** Indicate whether the record reflects current status, historical data, or an undated snapshot.

## Scoping Negative Observations

When an entity, profile, or attribute is not found:
- Avoid absolute negations (e.g. "The company has no LinkedIn profile").
- Scope the observation strictly to the evaluated query and sample:
  - "In the evaluated sample of LinkedIn search results for query X on date Y, no active corporate page was observed."
- Negative findings must document the exact query and parameters used.
